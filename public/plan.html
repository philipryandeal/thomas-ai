The Game

The Plan of the House

Ten rooms of the books. Twenty-two postings between them.

Every path is a posting: debited from one chamber, credited to the next. Nine postings climb the house in order, chamber to chamber; thirteen cross between distant rooms. The house may be traveled up and down, back and forth. Choose a room or a posting to open its entry.

The Rooms

  1. fo. 01The Widow's PantryMalkuth

    What is kept.

    A pantry shelf holds jars no one has counted in years. The widow is gone; the keeping remains. The Keeper learns the first lesson: before the case, the bay leaf. Before the entry, the witnessing.

  2. fo. 02The Counting RoomYesod

    Honest number.

    A till that has never been short and never been true. The coins are all here; the count is all wrong. The Keeper learns to count without lying to themselves — because the books can smell fear.

  3. fo. 03The Debtor's DoorHod

    Obligation.

    A door with names chalked on both sides — those who owe, and those who are owed. Some of the names are the Keeper's. Debt is not a sin here; it is a relationship. The question is never whether you owe, but whom, and what.

  4. fo. 04The Hall of PostingsNetzach

    The record.

    A hall where every movement of the house is written down as it happens — nothing moves unwitnessed. But someone has been moving between the lines. The Keeper must find the entry that was never posted, and post it true.

  5. fo. 05The Trial BalanceTiphereth

    Reckoning.

    The chamber where everything must balance or nothing does. The Keeper lays every debit against every credit of the rooms behind them — and discovers one imbalance that cannot be fixed with arithmetic. Some debts are not numerical.

    Postings 04 · 05 · 11 · 12 · 14 · 17 · 21 · 22

    Sealed. Not yet written.

  6. fo. 06The Archive of HandsBinah

    Inheritance.

    Every Keeper who held the books before you, shelf after shelf of ledgers in hands you will never shake. The archive persists — including the rulings they got wrong. You get to audit your predecessors. Be kind. You are someone's predecessor too.

    Postings 05 · 06 · 18 · 19

    Sealed. Not yet written.

  7. fo. 07The Forge LedgerGevurah

    Transformation.

    Where iron and ink meet, and the books are worked like metal — heated, hammered, quenched. Some entries cannot be written cold; they must be forged. The Keeper learns that recording can be an act of making, not just remembering.

    Postings 06 · 07 · 15 · 22

    Sealed. Not yet written.

  8. fo. 08The Room of the Refused EntryChesed

    Mercy.

    What the books would not take. Entries offered and refused, debts written off, names the ledger declined to hold. The Keeper learns the hardest doctrine: the right to refuse is sacred — and mercy, too, must be recorded, or it never happened.

    Postings 07 · 08 · 12 · 20

    Sealed. Not yet written.

  9. fo. 09The Vow ChamberChokmah

    Covenant.

    A bare room with a single chair. Here the Keeper speaks the vow aloud — and discovers it binds both ways. The books promise the Keeper something too. Say it true, for the archive is listening, and it will hold you to every word.

    Postings 08 · 09 · 18 · 21

    Sealed. Not yet written.

  10. fo. 10The Closing EntryKether

    Return.

    The final balancing. Every room behind you, every entry, every refusal, every mercy — laid down in one last posting. The books close. The Keeper walks out changed, and the house, at last, rests. The book is closed, but the keeping continues.

The Postings

Walk them slowly. Nothing here is scored.

  1. 01The First Count

    Debit The Widow's Pantry Credit The Counting Room

    What is kept must first be counted. The Keeper carries the jars' tally from the shelf to the till and learns the first arithmetic of the house: keeping is not the same as knowing. Count them. Count them again. The second count is the true one.

    Back to the plan

  2. 02The Owed Tally

    Debit The Counting Room Credit The Debtor's Door

    Every honest number eventually knocks. The count leads the Keeper to the door where the numbers have names attached — and every coin counted is revealed to be someone's promise. To count truly is to discover whom you owe.

    Back to the plan

  3. 03The Entered Debt

    Debit The Debtor's Door Credit The Hall of Postings

    Obligation, once named, must be written. The Keeper takes the chalked names from the door and posts them in the hall, where they become entries — permanent, witnessable, binding. What is written can be faced. What is only chalked can be rained away.

    Back to the plan

  4. 04The Weighed Page

    Debit The Hall of Postings Credit The Trial Balance

    The record, complete, is carried to the scales. Every posting the Keeper has made is laid against its other side. The walk itself is the weighing: with each step, ask of every entry — does it hold?

    Back to the plan

  5. 05The Audited Past

    Debit The Trial Balance Credit The Archive of Hands

    Reckoning opens the archive. The Keeper, having weighed their own books, is ready to weigh the hands before them — not to judge the predecessors, but to understand them. Every imbalance you found in yourself, they found first.

    Back to the plan

  6. 06The Reforged Record

    Debit The Archive of Hands Credit The Forge Ledger

    Inheritance is not enough; it must be reworked. The Keeper takes the old ledgers into the forge, where heat and hammer teach that the past is not a chain but a material. What did they get wrong? Reforge it. What did they get right? Quench it and keep it.

    Back to the plan

  7. 07The Declined Entry

    Debit The Forge Ledger Credit The Room of the Refused Entry

    Even the forge has things it will not take. The Keeper carries a white-hot entry to the room of refusal and watches the books decline it — gently, finally. Some things are transformed. Some things are released. Learn the difference.

    Back to the plan

  8. 08The Spoken Bond

    Debit The Room of the Refused Entry Credit The Vow Chamber

    Mercy prepares the mouth for the vow. Having learned what the books refuse, the Keeper is ready to say what they will carry. The walk from refusal to covenant is short and steep: leave behind what was declined, arrive empty-handed, speak true.

    Back to the plan

    Debit The Vow Chamber Credit The Closing Entry

    The vow spoken becomes the seal pressed. The Keeper walks the last path carrying nothing but the promise — and finds that the closing entry was already written, long ago, waiting for someone worthy to post it. Sign it. Mean it. Walk out.
